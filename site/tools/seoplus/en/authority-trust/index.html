Building recognition and earning Google's trust (and the AIs')

In short With equal content, Google ranks whoever it trusts most, and AIs cite whoever they know. That trust is built on four fronts: the proof of expertise visible on your website (E-E-A-T), your Google Business Profile for local anchoring, internal linking that distributes strength where it matters, and time, which you should count honestly rather than endure. Trust, the slow fuel of rankings

Two craftsmen publish the same "bathroom renovation" page. One ranks first, the other sits on page 3. The difference is almost never in the text: it is in everything around the text. Who is speaking? Do other websites recommend them? Are their reviews real and numerous? Does their website match their Google profile, their social accounts, the directories? That is authority: a bundle of proof that you are who you claim to be.

And the phenomenon has amplified: generative AIs, which recommend businesses in their answers, cross-check exactly the same signals before citing a name. Trust work now serves two counters at once.

The proof of expertise on your website: E-E-A-T

Google sums up its reading grid in four letters: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. Behind the acronym, very concrete proof that a small business can put up in a few days: author pages with real names and real backgrounds, verifiable client references, numbers from your own practice rather than copied ones, complete legal notices, a visible address and phone number.

The full list of proof, sorted by effort: E-E-A-T, how to prove your expertise to Google and the AIs.

The Google Business Profile: your second website

For a local business, the Google Business Profile often generates more contacts than the website itself: it fills the whole screen on mobile when someone searches your name, and the local pack shows above the classic results. It is also a pillar of consistency: identical name, address and phone everywhere (the famous NAP), well-chosen categories, recent reviews with replies.

The fields that actually matter and the mechanics of reviews: Google Business Profile, the priority fields.

Distributing strength: internal linking

The authority you earn lands somewhere, often on the homepage. Without internal linking, it stays there. The links between your pages are the distribution circuit: they push strength toward the pages that are fighting for queries, and they show Google which topics form your areas of expertise. It is exactly the silo logic that structures this blog: reference guides like this one, linked to their detailed articles, and back.

The hub and child pages method, anchor rules and classic mistakes: internal linking, the free technique nobody uses.

The time factor: count honestly

Authority is the only SEO pillar you can neither buy nor brutally accelerate. Technical fixes act in days, content in two or three months, trust in six to twelve months. That calendar is not a punishment: it is a barrier to entry that protects those who do the work. The competitor who started two years ago is protected by those two years; every month you keep the pace protects you in turn.

The detailed calendar, and how to measure progress without fooling yourself: how long does SEO take to produce results.

A website that just opened has no history, no links, no reviews: Google puts it on probation before giving it a chance.
